Concrete Quality Assurance and Non-Conformance Management

Keeping every sample traceable to its truck, batch and pour, so a result can be read fairly.

5.1

Scenario

A proposed concrete quality-management plan for the mall, whose PS1 deck estimates about 12,000 m³ of concrete. Supply, delivery records, inspection, sampling and test results are all linked.

No mix designs, cube results, slump readings, rejected loads or test registers were supplied.

5.2

Quality workflow

  1. Approve supplier & mix
  2. Check ticket: grade, batch time, qty
  3. Inspect fresh concrete; specified tests
  4. Record sample ID, pour, batch, time
  5. Cast, identify, cure, test
  6. Compare with specification
  7. NCR; engineer decides
  8. Close CAPA with evidence

5.5

Non-conformance

!Principle
One low cube result does not automatically mean demolition, and one passing cube does not prove the whole structure compliant.
5.6

Conclusion and reflection

A traceable QA/QC framework from delivery to testing and engineering review, not a report of tests actually carried out.

I learned that concrete quality control depends on traceability at every stage. A test result is meaningful only when it can be linked to the correct batch, pour, sample and curing record. I also understood that a non-conformance needs a structured review and engineering decision rather than an assumption based on one test value.
