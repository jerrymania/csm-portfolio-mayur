Material Planning, Steel and Formwork Wastage Control

Planning steel and formwork from the drawings, so that what is bought, cut, used and returned can be accounted for.

3.1

Scenario and objectives

Extend the seven-block mall into a material-efficiency study: plan reinforcement steel and formwork, coordinate procurement with block-wise construction, and track consumption and reusable offcuts.

  • Link reinforcement procurement to approved structural drawings and BBS
  • Reduce over-ordering and avoidable cutting waste
  • Organise steel by grade, diameter and length
  • Plan formwork reuse across similar members and work fronts
  • Reconcile receipts, issues, installed quantities, returns and scrap

No steel quantities, BBS, wastage baseline, purchase records or measured savings were supplied, and none are invented.

3.2

BBS workflow

  1. Latest approved drawings + revision register
  2. Separate by block, level, element
  3. Unique bar marks
  4. Diameter, shape, spacing, qty, cutting length
  5. Bends, hooks, anchorage per design / standard
  6. Member- and block-wise BBS
  7. Independent check
  8. Controlled schedules to fabrication
  9. Record issued, installed, returned, scrap
!No invented BBS
No bar quantities are calculated: there are no approved reinforcement drawings to work from.
3.3

Procurement and storage

Hybrid procurement Proposed

Project-wide demand forecasting and supplier arrangements, with purchase and delivery releases phased against the checked BBS and programme. Buffer stock only where lead time and supply risk justify it.

Steel storage

  • Raised supports, away from standing water and contamination
  • Segregated by grade, diameter, length and bar mark
  • Durable tags; controlled access
  • Receipts, issues, transfers, returns and scrap recorded
BalanceOpening stock + Receipts − Issues/consumption − Transfers out ± Approved adjustments = Closing stock

Define the wastage denominator before quoting a percentage. Reusable offcuts returned to controlled inventory aren't counted as scrap.

3.6

Conclusion and reflection

Drawing-based quantity planning, controlled procurement, careful storage and reconciliation can reduce avoidable material loss. No wastage percentage or saving is reported.

I learned that material efficiency begins during planning, before materials reach the site. A checked BBS helps convert structural details into procurement and fabrication requirements, while stock reconciliation helps identify where actual use differs from the plan. I also understood that reuse depends on inspection and proper handling, not simply on keeping old materials for another cycle.
