2.1

Problem and objective

Design a material-procurement system for a large project that runs with minimum day-to-day supervision from the project manager. The new store manager has no large-project experience, work starts in 15 days, and the site has two gates: one in, one out Source.

  1. Store layout for consumables, non-consumables, equipment and safety gear.
  2. Choose ingress and departure gates from the store location.
  3. Record every vehicle registration at entry and exit.
  4. Collect the delivery invoice; copies to security, store manager and accounts.
  5. Joint QC check by the QA/QC inspector and store manager, using the nearby lab. Rejected material leaves promptly with paperwork; accepted material goes to its designated slot.
SMART-TRACK combines physical site planning, controlled procedures, QR codes, cameras, digital records and alerts. Routine work is system-guided; the project manager handles exceptions Proposed.

2.6

Inspection and decisions

On arrival the store manager and QA/QC inspector are notified; the vehicle waits in the holding area without blocking the road. The store manager scans the QR, checks description, quantity and condition; QA/QC checks against approved specifications and sends samples to the adjacent lab when required. Photos, quantities, findings, sample references and test reports attach to the Delivery ID.

Rejected material goes straight back when it can be inspected safely without unloading. If it has to be unloaded or sampled, it is quarantined in Zone R until return. The "immediate exit" rule never bypasses safe handling or the inspection record; procurement and accounts are notified either way.

2.7

Gate 2 departure

  1. Driver shows the red or green exit pass.
  2. ANPR records registration and departure time.
  3. Vehicle record, Delivery ID and pass status are matched.
  4. Security investigates any mismatch before the vehicle leaves.
  5. Exceptions are recorded, including a vehicle leaving without delivering.
  6. The visit is closed after the checks.

If ANPR fails, plates are recorded manually.

!Matching is not authorising
An invoice on its own is not payment approval. Duplicate invoices, quantity differences and missing documents are flagged, and payment needs authorised human review.

Inventory records hold opening stock, receipts, issues, returns, transfers, approved adjustments and closing balances, with batch/lot, location and an audit trail. Low-stock alerts run off approved minimum levels.

Compliance and reflection

RequirementWhere SMART-TRACK answers it
Store layoutSix coded zones A, B, C, D, Q, R
Gate choiceGate 1 entry, Gate 2 exit, one-way route
Vehicle recordANPR at both gates; manual backup
Invoice copiesThree copies; Delivery ID and QR
QC checkJoint inspection, adjacent lab, rejection paperwork, storage confirmed by QR and inventory update

This problem statement helped me understand that material procurement is a complete control process rather than simply ordering and receiving supplies. Each delivery needs to be identified, inspected, accepted or held, stored and reconciled with the purchase order and invoice. The ACCEPT, HOLD and REJECT paths are important because they prevent unverified material from entering usable stock. I also learned that automation needs practical backup procedures and human approval for important quality and payment decisions.
