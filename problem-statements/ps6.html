Construction Progress Monitoring and Project Coordination

Finding what stops the next activity from starting, and measuring progress honestly.

6.1

Scenario and objectives

A project-control framework for the seven-block mall, coordinating block-wise work packages, labour, deliveries, equipment, access, inspections and reporting.

  • Develop a work breakdown structure
  • Link packages to a baseline programme
  • Monitor planned vs actual progress
  • Coordinate labour, materials and equipment
  • Spot constraints before they hit work fronts
  • Keep records of changes, risks and corrective actions

No baseline programme, progress reports, dates, quantities or costs were supplied.

6.2

Planning workflow

  1. Approved scope, drawings, milestones
  2. Block- and discipline-wise packages
  3. Predecessors and access constraints
  4. Logic-linked baseline
  5. Link procurement and labour
  6. Long-lead items and approvals
  7. Update at agreed interval
  8. Investigate variance; recover
  9. Rolling look-ahead

Two blocks at a time, from the separate site visit, is a planning option, not a confirmed programme.

6.6

Conclusion and reflection

This connects scope, schedule, procurement, labour, equipment, quality and safety. It's a proposed monitoring framework, not a report of site progress.

This case study helped me see that progress monitoring should identify what is preventing the next activity from starting or finishing. A comparison of planned and actual progress is useful only when the reasons for delay are investigated and responsibility for corrective action is clear. I also learned that physical progress, expenditure and payment status should be measured separately.
